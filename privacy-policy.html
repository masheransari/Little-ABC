1. Introduction

Little ABC – Learn, Trace & Color ("Little ABC", "the app") is an alphabet learning app for children aged 2–6. Children explore letters, listen to letter names and words, trace letters and color pictures.

This policy explains what information the app handles, what the advertising software inside the app collects, and the choices parents and guardians have. The app has no user accounts, no sign-in, no chat and no in-app purchases. The developer does not run any server that receives information from the app.

Important: learning progress and pictures stay on the device, but the app is not data-free. When the device is online, Google software in the app (advertising, usage analytics and crash reporting) collects certain device, diagnostic and interaction data, as described in sections 4–7.

3. Children's privacy

  • The app does not ask children for their name, age, photo, voice, e-mail address, location or any other personal information, and has no way for a child to enter or share such information.
  • The app does not use the camera or microphone and does not request location, contacts or photos permissions.
  • Links that leave the app (Google Play, e-mail, this policy online, sharing) are only available in the parent area, which is protected by a parent gate.
  • Advertising is configured for a child audience (see section 6).

If you believe information about your child has been handled in a way this policy does not describe, please contact the developer.

4. Information collected by the app and its SDKs

Kept only on the device (never sent to the developer)

  • Learning progress and rewards (section 8).
  • Parent settings (section 8).
  • Pictures the child colors and unfinished drafts (section 9).

Collected by third-party software in the app

The app includes these Google software development kits (SDKs):

  • Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob) – shows advertisements (sections 5–7).
  • Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) – shows Google's privacy and consent options where the law requires them (for example in the EEA and the UK).
  • Google Analytics for Firebase – anonymous usage statistics, such as how many devices use the app and which screens are opened, so the developer can improve it (section 7).
  • Firebase Crashlytics – reports when the app crashes, so the developer can fix the problem (section 7).

The app contains no social-media, sign-in or account SDKs, and does not send names, e-mail addresses, photos or any text typed by a user to these services.

5. Google AdMob and advertising practices

The app is free and shows banner advertisements from Google AdMob. Ads appear in a fixed, labelled "Advertisement" area at the bottom of some menu and parent screens. They are not shown during tracing, coloring, the listening game, the welcome screen or the parent gate.

Full-screen (interstitial) ads are switched off in the current version. If they are ever enabled, they would only appear at natural breaks (after an activity is finished), with frequency limits, and this policy would be updated first.

Ads are only requested when the device is online. Without a connection, no ad is requested and the advertising SDK sends no ad requests.

6. Child-directed, non-personalised advertising

Before any ad is requested, the app applies these settings to every ad request:

  • Child-directed treatment (AdMob "age-restricted treatment: child").
  • Maximum ad content rating G (suitable for general audiences).
  • Personalisation disabled: no interest-based advertising and no remarketing.
  • Under the age of consent: this is signalled to Google's consent platform.

The app uses the Google Mobile Ads SDK, which Google Play lists as a Families Self-Certified Ads SDK. The developer cannot control the content of each individual ad. If you see an ad you think is inappropriate, please report it.

7. Advertising, analytics and crash-reporting data practices

Google Mobile Ads SDK (advertising)

According to Google's published data disclosure for the Google Mobile Ads SDK, the SDK automatically collects and shares the following with Google when ads are requested:

Data typePurpose (as stated by Google)
IP address (may be used to estimate an approximate location)Advertising, analytics, fraud prevention

Diagnostic information (for example app launch time, hang rate, energy use)Analytics, fraud prevention
Device identifiers: the app set ID. The Android advertising ID is not available to the app because its permission is removed.Advertising, analytics, fraud prevention

Google states that this data is encrypted in transit. The Google consent platform (UMP) may also store and process consent information on the device and with Google. Google handles this data under its own policies:

  • Google Privacy Policy
  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services
  • Google Mobile Ads SDK data disclosure

Google Analytics for Firebase (usage statistics)

The app uses Google Analytics for Firebase only to understand how the app is used (for example the number of active devices, sessions and screens viewed) and to improve it. It is configured for a child audience:

  • the Android advertising ID is not collected (collection switched off, and its permission is removed);
  • advertising storage, advertising user data and ad personalisation are set to denied, so analytics data is not used for advertising or ad personalisation;
  • the app sets no user ID, user properties or custom data, and sends no names, e-mail addresses or other personal details.

According to Google's documentation, the SDK automatically collects:

Data typePurpose (as stated by Google)
App-instance ID (a random identifier for this installation of the app, not linked to a person)Counting users and sessions
Masked IP address, used to derive a coarse location (for example the country)Analytics reporting
App lifecycle events (for example first open, sessions, screen views) and basic device information (device model, operating-system version, app version)Analytics reporting

Firebase Crashlytics (crash reports)

If the app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics sends a crash report so the developer can fix the problem. According to Firebase's documentation, a report contains the technical stack trace, relevant app state at the time of the crash, device metadata (for example device model, operating-system version, memory and disk space) and a random Crashlytics installation identifier used to count how many devices are affected. The app adds no custom keys, logs or user IDs to crash reports.

Analytics and crash data are processed by Google under the Firebase privacy and security information and the Google Analytics data disclosure. Google states that this data is encrypted in transit.

The app does not request or transmit the SIM serial, build serial, IMEI, IMSI, MAC address, Wi-Fi SSID/BSSID, phone number or precise location.

8. Local learning progress and settings storage

The app saves the following in its private storage on the device:

  • Learning progress: letters explored and traced, listening-game results and rewards earned.
  • Parent settings: sound effects, voice, music, vibration, learning mode and letter display.

This information is used only to run the app (for example, "Continue learning" and reward stars). It is not sent to the developer. Parents can erase it in Parents › Settings › Reset Progress.

9. Saved artwork storage and deletion

  • Pictures the child colors, and unfinished drafts, are saved in the app's private storage. They are not saved to the photo gallery and are not uploaded by the app.
  • A picture can be deleted in My Artwork. Resetting progress keeps saved artwork; uninstalling the app removes it.

10. Data sharing and third-party services

  • The developer does not sell, rent or share information about users. The developer does not receive the data collected by the Google SDKs in a form that identifies your child.
  • Data collected by the Google Mobile Ads SDK, UMP, Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics is sent to Google as described in section 7. Analytics data is not linked to advertising (advertising consent types are set to denied).
  • Device backup: if Android backup is turned on for the device, Android may include the app's progress, settings and pictures in the device's backup to the Google account set up on the device. That backup is managed by Google and the device owner, not by the developer.
  • If a parent chooses Share App, Rate App, More Apps or Contact Us, the chosen app (for example Google Play or an e-mail app) opens and handles what the parent then does under its own policy. An e-mail sent to the developer contains only what the parent writes, plus the sender's e-mail address.

11. Data retention and deletion

  • On the device: progress, settings and pictures are kept until a parent resets progress, deletes pictures, clears the app's storage or uninstalls the app.
  • Google: data collected by Google's SDKs is retained by Google under its policies. See how Google retains data.
  • Crash reports: Firebase Crashlytics keeps crash stack traces and associated identifiers for 90 days, according to Firebase.
  • Usage statistics: Google Analytics keeps event-level data for the retention period set by the developer in Google Analytics.
  • E-mails to the developer: kept only as long as needed to answer the request. You can ask for an e-mail conversation to be deleted.

12. Security practices

  • Progress, settings and pictures are stored in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read on a normally configured device.
  • The parent area and all external links are behind a parent gate.
  • Legal pages shown inside the app are displayed from a bundled copy, with scripts disabled.
  • Google states that the data its advertising, analytics and crash-reporting SDKs collect is encrypted in transit.

No method of storage or transmission is completely secure. If you think there is a security problem with the app, please contact the developer.

13. Children's rights and applicable privacy requirements

Depending on where you live, privacy laws (for example the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act, the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation, or similar laws elsewhere) may give parents and guardians rights over a child's information, such as the right to ask what is held, to have it deleted, or to object to its use.

  • Information kept on the device can be reviewed and deleted directly in the app or by uninstalling it.
  • For data processed by Google, use Google's privacy controls or contact Google.
  • For anything else, e-mail asheransar@gmail.com. The developer will respond within a reasonable time. Because the developer does not hold information that identifies your child, some requests may need to be handled on the device or by Google.

The app is designed to follow the Google Play Families Policy. This policy describes the app's actual practices; it is not a statement that any particular law has been certified as met.
